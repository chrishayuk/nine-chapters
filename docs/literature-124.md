# Liu Hui, problem 8.18: literature check on 124 counting rods

Checked 24 November 2026.

**Conclusion: PARTLY KNOWN.** The interpretation of the 77/124 算 as numbers of counting rods is documented. A published numerical reconstruction also contains the coefficient board associated with the supplied 124-rod count. **An explicit identification of 124 as the maximum simultaneous rod occupancy on this path was NOT FOUND in what I could read.** The full text of Wei Xuegang and Guo Shirong (2016), the most important outstanding source, was not obtained. Consequently this is not a finding of novelty, nor a finding that their paper lacks the result.

Three different claims must be distinguished:

1. **Interpretation:** Liu Hui's 124 算 means 124 physical rods.
2. **Numerical reconstruction:** a particular sequence of column operations produces the relevant board.
3. **Peak identification:** counting the rods on that sequence, under a stated convention, gives a maximum of exactly 124, attained before the third column is discarded.

A source establishing (1), or printing a board relevant to (2), does not by itself establish that its author made claim (3).

## Zhu's 110

Zhu (2010, p. 82): "筆者經過計算，光擺放方程就要用去一百十根筭籌" (by my calculation, setting out the array alone uses 110 rods), followed at once by "平均而言，一位數字需要用2.9根筭籌" (on average a digit needs 2.9 rods), with note 29: "1 用一根，2、6 用兩根，3、7用三根，4、8用四根，5、9用五根，0不用筭籌" (1 takes one rod; 2 and 6 two; 3 and 7 three; 4 and 8 four; 5 and 9 five; 0 none). The 2.9 is the mean over the ten digits, (1+4+6+8+10)/10.

That is this repository's rule. Under it the opening board of 8.18 is 116 rods, column by column from the right 23, 24, 21, 23, 25 (coefficients 86, 實 30), and the count does not depend on how signs or empty places are shown, because none are present at the start.

What could make 110 (checked by script):

| Hypothesis | Count |
|---|---|
| Zhu's rule, counted digit by digit | 116 |
| 38 non-zero digits × 2.9 | **110.2** |
| all 39 digits, the 0 of 140 included, × 2.9 | 113.1 |
| 6–9 as d−5 rods | 105 |
| 5 as one rod | 92 |
| 0 as one rod | 117 |
| coefficients only, no 實 | 86 |
| one coefficient misread | cannot lose 6 rods |

The only single-figure changes that give 110 alter a 實 (128 to 1, 10 or 100; 95 to 4, 8 or 13…), none of them a plausible reading. The estimate is the one explanation that gives 110 exactly, it is the figure Zhu states in the next sentence, and he uses the same estimate for his example in note 30, six two-digit numbers (2.9 × 12 = 34.8, "近三十五根"). It remains an inference: Zhu does not show the count. It does not bear on the 124, which is counted digit by digit under the same rule, and the rule itself is confirmed by his note.

## Independent check of the supplied boards

**This section is my arithmetic check of the user's data, not a literature finding.** It does not purport to reproduce every stage of Guo's figure or to prove a maximum over every possible execution.

Let `r(n)` sum digit costs in `abs(n)`, with digit costs `0,1,2,3,4,5,2,3,4,5` for digits 0–9 respectively. Thus signs consume no rods. For example, `r(−137)=1+3+3=7`.

| Column, counted from the right | 麻 | 麥 | 菽 | 荅 | 黍 | 實 | Rods |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Right | −26 | 7 | −137 | 107 | 5 | 0 | 23 |
| 2nd | −25 | 6 | −124 | 101 | 3 | 0 | 21 |
| 3rd | 23 | −3 | 94 | −80 | −5 | 1 | 27 |
| 4th | −26 | 5 | −109 | 93 | 4 | 0 | 27 |
| Left | −22 | 3 | −90 | 77 | 5 | 3 | 26 |
| **Total** | | | | | | | **124** |

After subtracting three copies of column 3 from the left, the left becomes `−91, 12, −372, 317, 20, 0`. Its cost remains **26**, so the total remains **124** until column 3 is discarded. Discarding that column removes **27** rods, leaving **97**. The five original columns cost **23 + 24 + 21 + 23 + 25 = 116**.

For clarity, writing `C1` for right and `C5` for left, the supplied early path is:

```text
C3 ← C3 − C4
C1 ← C1 − 35 C3
C2 ← C2 − 32 C3
C4 ← C4 − 28 C3
C5 ← C5 − 23 C3
C3 ← C3 − C5        # first board tabulated above
C5 ← C5 − 3 C3      # second 124-rod board
discard C3
```

These are consolidated column transformations. Counting rods needed for temporary copies, digit-level multiplication/subtraction, or successive individual subtractions would require an additional execution model. A count of completed displayed states should be described as such, unless those other storage demands have also been checked.

## Assessment of priority

**PARTLY KNOWN** is the defensible overall verdict: published interpretation and numerical reconstruction overlap with the proposed observation. **NOT FOUND** applies narrowly to the explicit, rule-specified **124-rod peak identification**, in the material read here.

The decisive unresolved question remains whether **Wei & Guo 2016, pp. 10–17**, already count the physical rods on this same board/path, or another one. Their accessible introduction makes them a particularly strong candidate. Until their body is read, the appropriate formulation is: **“I have not located an explicit prior demonstration of this peak in the sources inspected; Wei and Guo (2016) remain unchecked beyond their opening.”**
